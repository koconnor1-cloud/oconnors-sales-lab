Getting started

Practice with a purpose

The Sales Lab is designed around a simple cycle: prepare, practice, review, and improve. Your instructor may assign a required scenario, or you can use Free Practice to build a specific skill.

1. Prepare

Read the buyer profile, scenario objective, product details, and assignment instructions before you begin.

2. Practice

Speak naturally with the AI buyer. Ask questions, listen to the response, and adapt instead of reading a script.

3. Review

Open your saved report to study the transcript, skill scores, coaching, pacing, and filler-word feedback.

4. Improve

Choose one skill to improve, try the scenario again, and compare the new report with your earlier attempt.

How the Sales Lab works
Home

See what is due, your next recommended action, and a snapshot of your progress.

Practice

Choose a buyer, scenario, and product for skill-building outside required coursework.

Assignments

Complete required activities, monitor attempts, and reopen saved reports. Recordings and transcripts are securely available to you and your instructor.

Performance

Track scores and skill development across your completed sessions.

Competition

Participate in separate class challenges when your instructor opens them. Competition activity is not the same as an assignment.

Core conversation flow
1
Opening & rapport

Earn attention, establish relevance, and create a professional reason to continue.

2
Discovery & listening

Use thoughtful open-ended questions, follow up on what matters, and avoid rushing to pitch.

3
Value proposition

Connect the solution to the buyer’s stated needs and explain the business impact clearly.

4
Objection handling

Acknowledge the concern, clarify its cause, respond with relevant value or evidence, and confirm resolution.

5
Closing & next steps

Recognize buying signals and confidently ask for an appropriate, specific commitment.

Eight scored skills

Scores reflect evidence in the conversation. A higher score requires consistent skill—not merely using one correct phrase.

Rapport

Professional opening, relevance, warmth, and credibility.

Discovery

Quality, depth, and sequencing of questions about needs.

Listening

Evidence that you understood and built on buyer responses.

Value proposition

Clear connection between solution, need, and measurable value.

Objection handling

Composure, clarification, relevance, and confirmation.

Clarity

Concise, organized language that is easy to understand.

Confidence

Decisive delivery without sounding aggressive or scripted.

Close

A timely, specific request for commitment or next action.

Searchable glossary
BATNABest Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement—the option you have if no deal is reached.
Buying signalA question or behavior suggesting the buyer is considering commitment.
Closed-ended questionA question usually answered with a short, specific response such as yes, no, or a number.
DiscoveryThe structured process of understanding the buyer’s goals, problems, priorities, and decision process.
FeatureA factual characteristic of a product or service.
Need-payoff questionA SPIN question that helps the buyer explain the value of solving a problem.
ObjectionA concern or barrier that prevents the buyer from moving forward.
Open-ended questionA question that invites explanation rather than a one-word response.
SPIN SellingA questioning framework using Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-payoff questions.
Trial closeA low-pressure question used to test readiness or uncover remaining concerns.
Value propositionA concise explanation of why a solution matters to this buyer and what result it can create.
Walk-away pointThe limit beyond which accepting a deal is worse than choosing your alternative.
